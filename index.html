Method & sources

How this was made.

  1. Rail network from U.S. Bureau of Transportation Statistics — North American Rail Network (NARN).
  2. Statewide parcel layer from Washington State Geospatial Open Data Portal.
  3. Python automation: spatial intersection of parcels with rail centerlines, manual review for false positives.
  4. Per-parcel attributes (owner, assessed value, acreage, assessor link) joined from county GIS data.
  5. Industry classification from county USEDESC field (15 categories), grouped here into 5 tiers for legibility.
  6. Rendered with MapLibre GL JS. Light basemap from CARTO Positron; satellite basemap from ESRI World Imagery. No tracking, no API keys, no third-party tokens.
  7. All statistics on this page are computed live from the loaded GeoJSON — no precomputation.

Notes

Why rail-served real estate matters.

A rail-served property is one where a freight rail spur extends from a Class I, regional, or short-line railroad onto private land — connecting the site directly to North America's freight network. The cost and complexity of building a new spur (right-of-way negotiation, track-grade engineering, FRA approvals) means the existing inventory is effectively fixed. New rail-served properties are not being created at scale.

This makes rail-served industrial real estate a scarce asset class. The 671 parcels here span $9.3 billion in assessed value across 562 parcels with recorded values, with concentrated ownership in agriculture (grain co-ops dominate) and heavy industry. Marine terminals are a small share of the parcel count but generate disproportionate freight volume — a single Port of Vancouver or Tacoma terminal can move more railcar loads than dozens of grain elevators combined.

Limits to acknowledge: 2022 snapshot, not a live feed. Assessed values are missing for 109 of 671 parcels. The intersection algorithm uses public NARN data and parcel centerlines — micro-sites where a spur enters at the property edge but does not extend onto the parcel itself may be undercounted. Industry classification follows county USEDESC codes and inherits any inconsistencies in those codes.
